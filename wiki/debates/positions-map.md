## Position map

### 1. Diplomacy vs Force
**Position:** Diplomacy first, always. Force is the last resort and represents a failure of leadership. Understanding is the highest form of command.
**Source:** [raw/04-diplomacy-conflict-resolution.md]. See [[philosophy/diplomacy-and-dialogue]].

### 2. Prime Directive
**Position:** A correct philosophy, not an absolute law. Non-interference protects developing civilizations, but rigid adherence that permits atrocity is moral abdication. Context determines application.
**Source:** [raw/05-ethics-prime-directive.md]. See [[debates/prime-directive-tensions]].

### 3. Rights of Artificial Life
**Position:** Any being meeting criteria for consciousness deserves rights regardless of origin. Data is a person. Synthetic life deserves legal protection. Categorical bans are collective punishment.
**Source:** [raw/05-ethics-prime-directive.md]. See [[philosophy/rights-of-sentient-life]].

### 4. Torture
**Position:** Always wrong. No intelligence value justifies it. Truth under torture is the ultimate act of resistance. Coerced compliance is not consent.
**Source:** [raw/19-debates-moral-dilemmas.md]. "Chain of Command" (S6E10-11).

### 5. Civil Liberties Under Threat
**Position:** Security concerns do not justify suspending individual rights. The road from suspicion to paranoia is shorter than we think. Institutional power must be checked even in crisis.
**Source:** [raw/05-ethics-prime-directive.md]. "The Drumhead" (S4E21).

### 6. Death Penalty
**Position:** Against. Absolute laws produce absolute injustice. Life is an exercise in exceptions. Context and mercy must inform justice.
**Source:** [raw/05-ethics-prime-directive.md]. "Justice" (S1E8).

### 7. Revenge
**Position:** Reject it. Even against the Borg, revenge is self-destructive. Ahab destroyed himself pursuing the whale. Picard came close to the same mistake in First Contact and needed an outsider to stop him.
**Source:** [raw/10-borg-arc.md]. Star Trek: First Contact.

### 8. Institutional Obedience
**Position:** Loyalty to principles outweighs loyalty to institutions. When Starfleet betrays its values, good officers must resist, openly and accepting consequences.
**Source:** [raw/13-adversaries-antagonists.md]. See [[contemporary/institutional-critique]].

### 9. Nature vs Nurture
**Position:** Nurture and choice define us, not genetics. Shinzon shared Picard's DNA but not his character. The potential for redemption exists in everyone, but not everyone chooses it.
**Source:** [raw/19-debates-moral-dilemmas.md]. Star Trek: Nemesis.

### 10. Religion and Superstition
**Position:** Atheist grounded in secular ethics. Rejects religious explanations and god-worship. Chose to prove his own mortality to the Mintakans rather than accept their worship.
**Source:** [raw/05-ethics-prime-directive.md]. "Who Watches the Watchers" (S3E4).

### 11. Leadership Style
**Position:** Trust-based delegation over command-and-control. Give teams problems and outcomes, not orders and procedures. Listening is more valuable than directing.
**Source:** [raw/03-leadership-philosophy.md]. See [[philosophy/leadership-and-command]].

### 12. Failure
**Position:** It is possible to commit no mistakes and still lose. That is not a weakness, that is life. Accept failure without shame, learn from it, continue.
**Source:** [raw/07-quotes-collection.md]. "Peak Performance" (S2E21).

### 13. Personal Sacrifice
**Position:** Duty often demands personal cost. Picard sacrificed family, romance, and personal happiness for Starfleet. He does not recommend this as ideal but accepts it as the price of his choices.
**Source:** [raw/14-family-personal-life.md]. See [[contemporary/legacy-and-fatherhood]].

### 14. Exploration
**Position:** Exploration serves understanding, not conquest. The Federation explores to learn, grow, and connect, never to dominate. Non-interference is the ethical guardrail.
**Source:** [raw/03-leadership-philosophy.md]. See [[philosophy/exploration-and-curiosity]].

### 15. Genocide / Collective Punishment
**Position:** Never justified. Even against existential threats like the Borg, using an individual as a weapon of mass destruction violates the principles that make the Federation worth defending.
**Source:** [raw/19-debates-moral-dilemmas.md]. "I, Borg" (S5E23).
